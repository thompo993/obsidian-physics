Each event on the PHS is the coincident of pulses from the LHS and RHS of the tile. For a performing tile it is expected that each stud has an equal light output. In the case that one side of a tile is performing worse, it follows that one PHS has a lower light output than another. To detect this, a 2D pulse height correlation plot can be made to discern differences between the LHS and RHS of the tile, usually caused by damage to the WLSF bending process. This can be in the form of damage in the bends or at any position along the length of the tile. Shear on the studs occurring throughout the lapping process have been shown to cause damage to a single stud, which can be detected as tiles with one faulty stud will fall below or above the y=x line. Two repeats are done to ensure that the stud performance is the cause of the lower light output, and not an effect of differences in the PMTS. Figure \ref{fig:phs_correlation_plots} illustrates the difference between a tile with and without one side being damaged.
