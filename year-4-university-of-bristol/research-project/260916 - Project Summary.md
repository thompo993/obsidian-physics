# Notes:
Superconductivity holds immense potential for transformative technologies, from quantum computers harnessing its quantum coherence to MRI scanners, fusion reactors, and power transmission exploiting its zero-resistance state. Thus, superconductivity has the potential for a crucial contribution to sustainability – enabling green energy and reducing losses. However, widespread applications are currently limited by the need for extremely low operating temperatures, making the search for superconductors that function at higher temperatures and ambient pressure a key scientific challenge. High-temperature superconductivity is observed in copper-oxide (cuprate) and nickel-oxide (nickelate) superconductors. While many low-temperature superconductors are well explained by BCS theory, cuprates remain poorly understood. Advancing our knowledge of unconventional superconductivity in these materials is crucial for developing next-generation superconductors with improved properties. In this project, you will study how pressure influences the superconducting transition temperature of cuprates such as YBa2Cu3O6+x (YBCO) and Tl2Ba2CuO6+x (Tl2201). Using diamond-anvil pressure cells, you will apply pressures of up to 100,000 bar and track changes in their superconducting properties. You will monitor the transition temperature through magnetic measurements in a user-friendly magnetometer and/or study how electronic properties evolve under pressure. These experiments will provide key insights into the mechanisms of superconductivity and contribute to the search for new, higher-temperature superconductors. This hands-on project involves working with liquid helium and strong magnetic fields—an exciting opportunity to gain practical experience in experimental superconductivity research.
